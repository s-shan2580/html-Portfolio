The Best Movies According To @yo_ohoii

My Top Three Movies Of All Time

Gangs Of Wasseypur-I & II

Gangs of Wasseypur grips you with its raw, fearless narration and never lets go. The story unfolds like real history—messy, brutal, and deeply human. Every character feels alive, brought to life by powerful performances that make you feel their anger, hunger for power, and desperation. The screenplay is sharp and relentless, filled with unforgettable dialogues and moments that hit hard. It doesn’t glamorize violence; it makes you feel its weight. By the end, you’re not just watching the film—you’re living inside its world, and that alone makes it unmissable.

Aankhon Dekhi

Aankhon Dekhi is a gentle, thought-provoking film about an ordinary man who decides to believe only what he sees and experiences himself. What starts as a simple idea slowly turns his life—and his family—upside down. Sanjay Mishra is brilliant as Bauji, bringing warmth, innocence, and quiet courage to the role. The film feels real, honest, and deeply human, with subtle humor and moments that stay with you. It doesn’t preach or shout. It simply makes you pause and ask: Do I truly live by my own understanding, or by borrowed beliefs?

Highway

Highway is an emotionally raw film that unfolds more through feeling than words. The narration is slow, intimate, and deeply personal, allowing the journey to mirror the inner transformation of its characters rather than chase a conventional plot. Alia Bhatt delivers a hauntingly honest performance, shedding layers of fear and vulnerability with remarkable ease, while Randeep Hooda brings quiet intensity and restraint to his role. Their chemistry feels natural, making every shared silence as powerful as the dialogues. The screenplay is subtle and reflective, letting emotions breathe instead of forcing drama. By the end, Highway leaves you with a strange ache and a sense of freedom, making it a film that doesn’t just tell a story—it makes you feel one.
